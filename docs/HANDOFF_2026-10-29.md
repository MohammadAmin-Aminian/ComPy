## What was done

1. Merged `claude/compliance-yv` (2.2) with `inversion-improvements`.
2. Audited the old inversion code, findings V1–V8, A1–A4 and F1–F2 in `docs/INVERSION.md`:
   - the v1 roughness term did nothing;
   - the v1 likelihood used the norm, not its square;
   - the v1 Vp and density coupling contradicts the paper text;
   - the v1 moves were asymmetric;
   - the error bars were too large;
   - pre-2.0 compliance dropped the wave-attraction term;
   - the v2 default `alpha=0.25` is a 4 m/s jump prior;
   - the published figure code has errors (F1–F2 under "Open").
3. Wrote `compy_inversion`:
   - Zha & Webb (2016) parameterization;
   - full data covariance;
   - DRAM sampler with Gibbs smoothing and parallel tempering;
   - sampled gain (`gain_bounds`) and joint epochs (`ComplianceData.join`);
   - free Vp/Vs;
   - kernels;
   - CRUST1.0 reader;
   - Numba forward model.
4. Ran all 8 YV stations with variants. The final run uses:
   - free crustal Vp/Vs;
   - the gain sampled within ±5 % per epoch;
   - the after-jump data (own Rayleigh gain) joined for RR36, RR38, RR40 and RR50;
   - tempering;
   - Z/P as the estimator.
5. Made figures in the paper's style (`scripts/i_paper_figures.py`): Fig. 9 heat maps, misfit function, Figs. 8 and 10, and thesis Fig. 3.19.

## Key results

- **Final runs.** All converge (R-hat ≤ 1.017), and 7 of 8 stations fit with χ² < N.
- **Gain.** RR50's after/stable gain from the inversion is 1.677, against 1.705 from Rayleigh waves, so the calibration is confirmed.
- **RR38.** It misfits. Its two epochs differ in shape, and 1/(P/Z) fits it better than Z/P, so its pressure channel is suspect. Leave it out of the interpretation.
- **Vp/Vs.** The data hardly constrain it. Fixed CRUST1.0 Vp, as in the paper, fits RR50 and RR52 poorly.
